Curatorial Introduction · Indian Knowledge System

Rediscovering India's Glorious Heritage

Indian Marvels is an interactive digital museum created under the Indian Knowledge System (IKS) curriculum at Walchand College of Engineering, Sangli, celebrating India's timeless mastery across architecture, structural engineering, acoustics, optics, astronomy, and sculpture.

From the monolithic basalt excavation of Kailasa Temple at Ellora and the lathe-turned soapstone star shrines of the Hoysalas, to the acoustic granite musical pillars of Hampi and the masonry solar observatories of Jaipur, each monument stands as a living treatise in geometry, material science, and artistic devotion.

Jantar Mantar · Jaipur

Samrat Yantra & Precision Timekeeping

The Brihat Samrat Yantra is the world's largest stone equinoctial sundial, rising 27 meters high. Its hypotenuse (gnomon) is inclined at 26.92°—matching Jaipur's exact latitude—so it points parallel to Earth's rotational axis. As the Sun moves across the sky, the gnomon casts a crisp shadow along two curved marble quadrants calibrated to measure local solar time down to 2 seconds.

Spherical Astronomy · Jaipur

Jai Prakash & Ram Yantra

Jai Prakash Yantra consists of two complementary sunken marble hemispheres representing an inverted map of the celestial dome. Crosswires suspended above cast a shadow inside the bowl, revealing the Sun's exact zodiac sign, declination, and right ascension, while carved passages allow astronomers to walk directly inside the hemisphere.

Ram Yantra comprises two open cylindrical structures with a central vertical gnomon and radial stone sectors that accurately measure celestial altitude and azimuth.

Architectural Optics · Hampi

The Pinhole Camera Effect at Virupaksha Temple

Inside a darkened inner hall behind the main sanctum of the Virupaksha Temple, sunlight enters through a small, narrow stone aperture facing the towering 50-meter eastern Raya Gopuram. Because light travels in straight lines (rectilinear propagation), rays from the pinnacle of the tower pass downward while rays from the base pass upward, casting a remarkably crisp, inverted silhouette of the gopuram onto the rear stone wall.

Lithophonic Acoustics · Hampi

Musical Pillars of Vijaya Vittala Temple

The Ranga Mantapa of Vijaya Vittala Temple features 56 sculpted monolithic granite columns—known as SaReGaMa Pillars. Each main shaft is surrounded by slender colonnettes carved from resonating granite rich in silica and metallic minerals; when gently tapped, they emit distinct classical Indian musical notes (swaras) resembling percussive and stringed instruments.

Click or tap any granite pillar to hear its resonant swara frequency.
